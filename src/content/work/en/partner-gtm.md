## From announcing an integration to driving acquisition, reactivation and partner-led growth

The company was preparing to launch a full integration with one of the main practice management systems used by private clinics in Australia.

We could have treated it like a standard product launch: update the website, publish a post and inform existing customers.

But when I looked at the opportunity, **I saw much more behind it**.

We already had active customers using that software, churned customers who now had a real reason to come back, and a group of almost 200 prospects in HubSpot that we knew were using the platform.

The partnership also gave us access to the partner's own channels and user base.

Instead of thinking only about how to announce the integration, I built a strategy around how we could use it to generate demand, reactivate accounts and create a new acquisition channel through the partner.

**A different strategy for each audience**

I divided the launch into four groups: active customers already using the software, churned customers, prospects identified in HubSpot and users from the partner's platform.

Sending the same message to everyone would not have worked.

Active customers needed to understand what was changing, while also giving us an opportunity to ask for referrals. Churned customers needed a clear reason to restart the conversation. Prospects needed to see that the product now worked directly with the system they already used every day.

To reach the partner's audience, we also needed to create materials their team could share through their own channels.

From there, I built the full launch plan: audience, message, channel, owner and timing.

**Reactivating existing demand**

The first phase focused on the audiences we could reach directly.

For active customers, we prepared a short email explaining the integration and launched a referral campaign aimed at other specialists using the same software.

For churned customers, we created a reactivation campaign using SMS and Sales follow-up. The message was adapted to the reason each account had stopped using the product.

We did not want to send another generic "just checking in" message with no real value.

The integration gave us a genuine reason to restart the conversation. It removed a specific workflow friction that may previously have slowed down adoption or the buying decision.

For the prospects already identified in HubSpot, we built an outbound strategy based on calls and a follow-up email sequence.

The message did not try to explain everything the platform could do. It focused on one simple point: they could now use it within the workflow they were already familiar with.

**Turning the partner into a distribution channel**

The second part of the strategy focused on the partner's audience.

I created the materials they needed to communicate the integration through their own channels, including:

- content for their website and newsletter;
- banners and campaign assets;
- an update to their integrations directory;
- and shared materials for prospects and customers.

This meant the launch did not depend only on our own channels.

We could reach relevant clinics through a company they already knew, used and trusted.

That was one of the most valuable parts of the project: the integration was not only a product improvement. It also allowed us to turn the partner into a new distribution channel.

**Preparing Sales to convert the interest**

The launch also needed to make the Sales team's job easier.

We created an interactive demo, a customer story, SDR messaging, follow-up sequences, a one-pager and a dedicated integration page.

The goal was to help Sales answer the questions that mattered most: How does it work? What changes in the workflow? Who is already using it? Why should I consider it now?

This made the conversation much more relevant to the prospect. Instead of a general product demo, Sales could show how the product fitted into the way they already worked.

**Result**

The campaign generated **more than 40 demos** within the prioritised audience and converted **more than 20 prospects into new customers**, with a demo-to-customer conversion rate of close to 60%.

We also reactivated several churned customers.

In total, the launch generated **more than $50K in new and reactivated revenue**.

But the main result was proving that an integration launch could be much more than a product update.

By segmenting the opportunity, reactivating existing demand, using the partner as a distribution channel and preparing Sales to convert the interest, we created a repeatable model for future launches.

The integration became a new growth channel.
