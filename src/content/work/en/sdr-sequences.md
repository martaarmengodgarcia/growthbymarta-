## From individual follow-ups to a scalable outbound system

When I joined Medow, the outbound process was fairly simple: an SDR would call a clinic and, if there was interest, send a follow-up email.

The problem was not a lack of effort. It was the lack of a consistent process.

Each SDR decided what to write, when to follow up and how many times to try. Email quality varied across the team and, when someone did not reply, it was easy to make another two or three calls without knowing whether there was any real interest.

There was a lot of activity, but the outcome depended too heavily on each person's individual judgement.

**What I did**

I designed and implemented a new outbound system in HubSpot for a team of eight SDRs, combining calls, emails and LinkedIn across multiple touchpoints.

I did not want to create one generic sequence for every account. Instead, I built different outreach paths based on the clinic's context:

- whether it had a full integration, partial integration or no integration;
- which competitor or solution it was using;
- which problem had come up during the first call;
- which value proposition was most relevant in that situation.

Each sequence included pre-written messaging and personalisation fields, so SDRs could incorporate what they had learned during the conversation.

This kept the outreach relevant and human, while removing the need to write every email from scratch or constantly decide what the next step should be.

Before rolling it out across the team, I tested the system with one SDR.

I monitored replies, demos booked and the points where accounts stopped progressing. Based on those results, I adjusted the messaging, timing and mix of touchpoints before scaling it to the rest of the team.

I then documented the process, rolled it out across the team and trained the other seven SDRs on which sequence to use, what to personalise and how to handle each type of response.

**Impact**

Comparing the same one-month period before and after the rollout:

| Metric | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accounts worked per month | 700 | 740 |
| Demos booked | 46 | 72 |
| Demo conversion rate | 6.6% | 9.7% |

The team worked just **40 additional accounts**, but generated **26 more demos**.

This resulted in:

- **57% more demos**
- **47% improvement in demo conversion**
- more capacity to work new accounts

The improvement did not come from better emails alone.

By introducing multiple touchpoints, more prospects responded at some point in the sequence, including those who confirmed they were not interested.

That response was still valuable. It allowed the team to close the account, avoid unnecessary follow-up calls and spend more time on new opportunities.

The result was a shift from a follow-up process that depended heavily on each individual SDR to a shared system used across the whole team: more consistent, easier to measure and still flexible enough to maintain personalisation.
