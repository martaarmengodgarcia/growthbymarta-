## From four standalone stories to a customer proof system

When I joined Medow, the company only had four customer stories.

The problem was not just that there were too few. They also failed to represent many of the customer profiles, problems and objections that came up during the sales process.

Sales could be speaking to a prospect who looked very similar to one of our existing customers, but have no relevant story to show that we had already solved the same problem.

I saw a clear opportunity: expand the customer story library, but build it around what the commercial team actually needed. The goal was not simply to produce more content. It was to create the stories most likely to help move opportunities forward.

**What I did**

I launched a two-month sprint to identify and produce the stories with the greatest potential value for the business.

I started by analysing our customer base and pipeline in HubSpot to understand:

- which customer profiles appeared most frequently;
- which problems and objections kept coming up;
- which segments accounted for the most opportunities;
- and which types of customers still had no relevant story behind them.

From there, I prioritised the right accounts, worked with Customer Success to approach the customers and led the interviews.

I was not simply looking for positive testimonials. I wanted stories that Sales could actually use: a recognisable problem, a clear change after adopting the product and concrete results.

Within two months, **we went from four to twelve customer stories**.

But each interview became more than one piece of content. I turned every story into a set of resources that could be used across the funnel and through different channels:

- a full customer story for the website;
- a PDF designed to be shared with opportunities;
- a HubSpot snippet with the message and link ready to send;
- quotes and results for emails, campaigns, LinkedIn and presentations;
- and materials partners could share directly with their own audiences.

The idea was to get as much value as possible from every story, rather than publishing it on the website and considering the job finished.

**Making sure the stories were actually used**

Creating more content would mean very little if Sales did not know which story to use or where to find it.

That is why I developed an **AI-powered Case Study Finder**: an internal tool where an SDR could enter the context of an opportunity — the type of customer, their problem or their main objection — and receive a recommendation for the most relevant story.

Instead of manually searching through different pages or relying on the same customer story every time, the team could find the strongest piece of proof for each conversation in seconds.

**Impact**

Within two months:

| Metric | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Published customer stories | 4 | 12 |
| Customer profiles represented | 3 | 8 |
| Opportunities that received a new story | 0 | 80+ |

The new customer stories were used across **more than 80 sales opportunities** during the first two months.

Opportunities that received a relevant customer story were **32% more likely to progress** to the next pipeline stage than those where no story was shared.

Not because a customer story could close a deal on its own, but because it made it much easier to show that Medow had already solved a similar problem for another specialist.

The project turned eight interviews into a reusable content library for Marketing, Sales and partners: website content, campaigns, emails, newsletters, PDFs, presentations and sales opportunities.

And with the Case Study Finder, the team could identify the most useful story for each conversation in seconds.

The result was not simply a larger collection of customer stories.

It was a system for creating them with intent, repurposing them more effectively and making sure they reached the conversations where they could have the greatest impact.
