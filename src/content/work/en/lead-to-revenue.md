## From measuring channels to understanding what was driving revenue

At Blip, we already had a lot of data in HubSpot: leads, opportunities, customers and funnel performance.

What was missing was a clear way to connect those results with investment by channel and compare performance consistently month over month.

Knowing how many leads had come from paid search, an event or a paid social campaign was useful, but it was not enough.

We needed to understand what happened next: how each channel converted from lead to opportunity and from opportunity to customer, how much value it ultimately generated, and how its efficiency changed as investment increased or decreased.

To answer those questions, I built a reporting system in Excel that combined HubSpot data with monthly investment by channel.

**How I built it**

I organised paid search, events, organic, paid social, email marketing and other initiatives using the same framework.

For each channel, we could see qualified leads, opportunities, customers, conversion rates between stages, investment, cost per result, contract value, and monthly and quarterly performance.

The aim was not to replace the HubSpot dashboards, but to add a layer of analysis that made it easier to interpret how each acquisition source was performing.

This allowed us to see whether a channel was generating strong volume but converting poorly into opportunities, whether another was producing fewer leads but higher-value contracts, or whether an increase in spend was creating real growth or simply more activity at the top of the funnel.

![Lead-to-Revenue reporting model in Excel](../../../assets/work/lead-to-revenue-visual-1.png)

**A system that became more valuable every month**

Each month, I extracted and validated the latest HubSpot data, updated investment by channel and compared the results with previous periods.

Using the same structure consistently allowed us to identify changes in conversion quickly and understand where an issue was emerging.

For example, we could see if a channel was maintaining lead volume but converting fewer leads into opportunities, if cost per opportunity was increasing, or if a fall in customers was caused by weaker acquisition or a problem further down the funnel.

The model worked as an early warning system. Instead of simply noticing that a channel was performing worse, we could understand what had changed, at which stage and when the change had started.

It also helped us account for the length of the B2B sales cycle. A lead generated in one month could take several months to become an opportunity or customer, so judging channels only on immediate results could lead to the wrong conclusions.

The system helped us separate three things that are often grouped together: **volume, quality and value**.

**From reporting to growth decisions**

The model became a shared reference point for Marketing, Sales and Leadership.

It allowed us to compare channels using the same criteria, identify where conversion was dropping and connect changes in performance with investment, seasonality or active campaigns.

This gave the team more context when discussing budget allocation.

The decision was no longer simply to invest more in the channel generating the most leads. It was about understanding the role each acquisition source played within the funnel and what it was contributing to pipeline and revenue.

![Channel performance breakdown](../../../assets/work/lead-to-revenue-visual-2.png)

**From monthly reporting to an annual view**

By maintaining the same structure throughout the year, I was able to use the historical data to produce the annual EMEA marketing performance report.

This allowed us to analyse how conversion, contract value and the role of each channel had evolved over time, rather than looking at each month in isolation.

More than presenting final numbers, the system helped explain what was changing within the funnel, what might be driving those changes and where it made sense to act.

**Result**

The result was a consistent view of marketing performance, from initial investment through to pipeline and revenue generated.

The model helped us understand how our acquisition sources were really working and the role each one played within the wider strategy: which channels created volume, which converted more effectively and which generated higher-value opportunities.

Instead of looking only at which channel produced the most leads, we could answer a much more useful question for the business:

**Which channel works best for what, and where does it make the most sense to keep investing?**
