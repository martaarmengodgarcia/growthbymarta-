## The challenge

Blip sold the same platform across very different industries, including education, travel, healthcare, retail, insurance and technical services.

**The product was horizontal, but the buying context was not.**

Each industry had its own challenges, buyers, objections and moments of stronger demand. Education became more relevant during student recruitment and enrolment periods. Travel, ahead of peak season. Other sectors became more active around industry events, market shifts or specific investment cycles.

Instead of communicating the same message to everyone throughout the year, we prioritised one or two verticals each quarter and focused Marketing and Sales efforts around them.

**How we built each vertical**

For each industry, we analysed the market timing, the buyer's main pain points, the processes creating the most friction, the most relevant use cases and the most common sales objections.

From there, we built a full go-to-market campaign.

The central asset was **an industry-specific MOFU whitepaper**. It was not generic content about WhatsApp. It showed how the platform could be used across the customer journey, using real pain points, conversation examples and clear automation opportunities.

In education, for example, we showed how a university could use WhatsApp to attract prospective students, answer questions, collect documentation, send reminders and support the journey through to enrolment.

The aim was to remove the need for the buyer to imagine how the product might fit their business. They could see it applied directly to their own processes.

**Coordinated activation**

Each vertical was activated through a connected mix of channels.

We ran Meta campaigns to promote the whitepaper, supported by industry-specific landing pages and creative. Leads then entered a HubSpot nurture flow, while we also activated our existing database through email campaigns segmented by sector.

The same work was used to prepare the commercial team.

We trained SDRs and Sales on the buyer, their main challenges, the priority use cases, the most common objections and the messages that were most likely to resonate.

This meant Marketing was not generating demand with one narrative while Sales went to market with another. **The whole team worked from the same positioning.**

**Events and credibility**

To strengthen each vertical, we developed industry-specific customer stories and chose one direct way to engage with the market.

Depending on the sector, we either attended one of its main industry events or organised a sector-specific breakfast with Meta, our partner, and a group of prospects.

These sessions combined industry trends, WhatsApp and conversational AI use cases, and more direct conversations with potential customers.

This allowed us to bring positioning, content, demand generation, partnerships and sales conversations together within one strategy.

**Impact**

The prioritised verticals performed better than equivalent generic campaigns.

Approximately, we saw:

- **30% more demos** in the sectors we focused on;
- **a 20% increase in lead-to-demo conversion**;
- **2–3x higher engagement** in industry-segmented email campaigns;
- and a stronger presence of those verticals within the pipeline.

The most important outcome, however, was **building a repeatable way to enter new markets**.

We selected the opportunity, understood the buyer, defined the positioning, and aligned content, paid, email, Sales, partners and events around the same vertical.

Rather than running isolated campaigns by sector, we created a more structured way to answer a key go-to-market question:

How do we make a horizontal product feel relevant, credible and easier to buy within each industry?
